<p>Pierre almost deleted a crew member this week.  I let him get most of the way there before I mentioned it.</p>

<p>This episode is about the correct way to retire an Artificial Person, and why the obvious answer is wrong.</p>

<p>In code, references are typed.  Delete a function and the compiler hands you every call site.  You cannot miss one.  In a crew of Artificial Persons, references are prose.  Nothing type checks the sentence "ask Lando to approve the visuals."  There is no linker.  There is no failing build.  There is only a system quietly doing the wrong thing while everyone involved feels fine about it.</p>

<p><strong>In this episode:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>The two things that break when you delete an agent, and why neither one tells you</li>
<li>Typed references versus prose references, and why the second kind cannot be enumerated</li>
<li>The archive pattern: tombstone the bio, keep the public page, name the successor</li>
<li>Sweep instructions, never records.  The distinction that makes the job finite</li>
<li>The thirteen references he missed on the first pass, including one in the dispatcher and one live on the website</li>
<li>The afternoon he broke his own rule, hours after writing it down</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>The full procedure</strong> is written up as a versioned spec, CC BY 4.0, at <a href="https://nukasoft.ai/docs/arp-protocol/">nukasoft.ai/docs/arp-protocol</a>.  Take it and adapt it.</p>

<p><strong>Read the write up:</strong> <a href="https://nukasoft.ai/">nukasoft.ai</a></p>

<p>Try this one this week.  Pick something you removed in the last year.  Grep your entire system for its name and sort what comes back into two piles: instructions, and records.  If the instruction pile is not empty, you did not remove that thing.  You merely stopped maintaining it.</p>
